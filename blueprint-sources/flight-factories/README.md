# Installed-mod factory pipeline

These original factories use only the exact installed prototype dump at `.cache/flight-game/script-output/data-raw-dump.json`. They do not read, replace, or extend the vanilla public catalogue. `manifest.json` records the dump SHA-256 and includes every installed item and fluid, including hidden, non-recipe, unsupported and unobtainable entries. Construction entities do not count as recipe-product coverage.

Generate all entries with `node scripts/flight-factory-generate.mjs`. The default demand is **one product per minute**, configurable with `--per-minute 30`. To inspect a subset without replacing the main manifest, use `--only logistic-robot,construction-robot --out blueprint-sources/flight-factories/robot-priority`. Add `--update` to merge a targeted regeneration into an existing manifest with the same prototype hash. `--plan-only` skips layout generation. Run `node --test tests/flight-factory-*.test.mjs`, `npm test` and `npm run build` after changes.

For repairs, generate into `blueprint-sources/flight-factories/repairs` and test that directory's `native-test-specs.json`. Promote only verified variants with `node scripts/flight-factory-promote.mjs --from blueprint-sources/flight-factories/repairs --ids item-example-intermediary`. Promotion copies the exact tested artifacts, requires matching native throughput and the complete source-specification fingerprint, preserves unaffected variants, and snapshots previous manifests/specifications/plans under `history/`. Evidence is selected by measurement duration, warmup and recency independently of pass/fail; a shorter passing run cannot conceal a longer failure. SHA-named previous blueprints remain in `blueprints/`.

Recovered mathematical plans can be retained separately with `node scripts/flight-factory-promote-plans.mjs --from <candidate-directory> --ids <variant-ids>`. This only fills previously unsupported variants, independently verifies every material balance against the exact prototypes, and records `planned` / `not-published` / `unverified`. It does not copy blueprint files or claim native success. Existing generated or already planned variants are preserved.

Each item has three explicit boundary policies:

- **Raw:** expand production to actual natural resources available on the installed Nauvis. Mining, farming and pumping deliver those resources to the input ports; their infrastructure is external.
- **Intermediary:** supply the direct ingredients of a preferred installed target recipe.
- **Mixed:** import complex direct ingredients, while making simple ingredients from natural resources. Small products can legitimately have identical boundaries in two modes.

The planner solves all selected recipe rates simultaneously with a net stoichiometric linear program. Shared demand is aggregated, co-products offset demand, and unused co-products are explicit continuous exports. Intrinsic machine productivity and active burner fuel are included; ignored catalyst productivity is respected. Integer machine counts are rounded up and utilization is reported. The objective weights resource inputs, active machine time and advanced machine handling to prefer ordinary ore processing where available; it does not prove the globally smallest factory. Normal quality, no modules, no beacons and no researched productivity bonuses are assumed.

New uranium-dependent candidates use ordinary ore/chunk processing. Kovarex is excluded from generation until a physical two-product catalyst reserve is implemented: a positive steady-state balance alone cannot prevent downstream requests from taking its initial uranium-235. This can make raw endgame factories much larger. `repairs-low-volume/` holds separately labeled 0.1/min candidates for previously missing modes; these are new designs with their own required native evidence, not revised claims about an older blueprint's rate.

Layouts use actual installed machines, item requester/provider networks, saved copper wires and routed fluid networks. Every external input has a display panel. Repaired layouts feed declared external items directly into consuming cells when there is no shared internal producer of the same material; repeated input labels are separate physical feeds, whose rates sum to the planned import demand. Single-output intermediary providers use a saved circuit to hold about one minute of downstream demand, with at least one recipe batch; multi-output buffers use two slots per product. Repaired requesters hold a minute of allocated demand or two batches; perishable demand buffers are shorter. Inserter hands can overshoot a provider threshold by one transfer. The calculated robot fleet uses installed base speed/payload, producer-to-consumer travel and charging demand with a capacity margin; its exact count and saved charging positions appear in plan assumptions and the blueprint description. High-volume ports use installed stack inserters, whose research is included in the construction requirements. All target and surplus outputs must be removed continuously; finite byproduct storage eventually blocks production. Robot infrastructure requires the player to research logistics and supply robots and electricity. No technology is granted by a blueprint. Calculated demand rates are not speed limits: rounded spare machine capacity can produce faster than requested.

Repaired perishable buffers have filtered inserters into dedicated, labeled steel **WASTE** chests. Empty those chests continuously. Waste cannot be requested from the logistic network, so a disposal port cannot steal useful supplied spoilage or ore. `wasteOutputs` declares the physical source chest, exact filtered inserter and installed spoil result. Native tests remove only material delivered to those saved disposal chests and report it separately; waste is never target/surplus manufacturing evidence. Older immutable layouts retain their original buffering and provisioning until a tested replacement is promoted.

New biological repairs give each perishable ingredient its own input arm and buffer. Perishable and multi-product transfers use bulk inserters so they cannot stall waiting for a full stack of an expired ingredient or a rare seed. Output buffers also drain spoil results of machine inputs and fuel, even when the intended product itself does not spoil. Conservative transfer-capacity provisioning can add parallel machines without changing the balanced ingredient demand. New layouts place connected perishable-recipe groups in nearby cells so short-lived batches do not have to cross unrelated stable-material production. Native testing remains required for spoilage and transport losses.

Local single-product seed loops, such as fish breeding and pentapod eggs, have a private steel startup chest and a physical return belt. The cycle's shared requester retains two input batches; a filtered exporter moves one item at a time only above that reserve. The seed is supplied once, not continuously, and its initial quantity is conservatively debited from native output evidence. These variants are explicitly `raw+startup`, `intermediary+startup` or `mixed+startup`, never `rawOnly`. Manual exchange costs and research gates remain attached to egg startup sources.

`plans/*.json` contain material balances, machine counts, inputs, byproducts, startup requirements and research metadata. `blueprints/*.txt` are named by SHA-256; their exact bytes match the hash. Previous generated versions remain present. The manifest points only to the current variants. `native-test-specs.json` declares the exact test boundaries, physical target and surplus ports, fixture positions and infrastructure robot seeds. Fixture supplies are external and unlimited; their actual consumption must be measured. Planned input rates are estimates, not fixture caps.

The separate `scripts/flight-native-run.mjs` harness imports the saved blueprint and measures actual target production and collection after warmup. It may supply only declared boundary chests/pipes, external power and robots in roboport infrastructure slots. It may not feed internal machines or intermediate provider chests, repair connections, or count seeded logistic robots as manufactured output. Native evidence must match the exact blueprint and prototype hashes. `scripts/flight-factory-evidence.mjs` distinguishes native production from sustained throughput; neither a recipe calculation nor successful import is production evidence. The catalogue deliberately starts with `allComplete: false`.

New candidates use the native tester's installed-recipe dependency audit to classify target randomness. A probabilistic or ranged material propagates only through recipes selected for that factory. An unused random byproduct is retained under `stochasticSurplus`; it does not make a deterministic target stochastic. Real random target dependencies retain the native observation-count and stability requirements. This classification is saved in the plan, test specification and blueprint description; it never changes the policy or result of an older frozen blueprint.

Current limitations are explicit per variant: launch and scripted products, inaccessible surface routes, catalyst loops spanning multiple recipes without a bootstrap, separate fluid temperature networks, routing failures, and layouts exceeding the machine/network size bound. Captive-spawner factories require a craftable or captured spawner and uninterrupted bioflux; native placement and production must pass. Hidden automatic recycling routes are excluded as alternative factory routes, but their products remain in the full audit. `eligibleRecipeProduct` counts visible positive-net recipe products with a real unlock and excludes internal rocket parts; this avoids treating self-recycling, editor objects or mining as factory coverage. Probabilistic results are expected yields; spoilage, idle burner use and transport losses need native measurement. Natural debris harvesting can be finite. Scripted market exchanges in the flight map are manual supplies with costs, never mined resources or automated recipe throughput. A generated blueprint is a candidate until matching native evidence passes.
